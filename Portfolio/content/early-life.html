Roots, Resiliency, & Small-Town Grit

Before the competitive intensity of Kota, the labs of IIT Jodhpur, or the case discussion rooms of IIM Calcutta, my world was bounded by the quiet contours of Sheopur—a modest town in the Chambal region that was granted official district status only after I was born. Growing up with limited formal educational infrastructure, learning wasn't about relying on elite coaching or specialized resources; it was about curiosity, self-drive, and making the most of what was in front of us.

My early schooling was rooted at St. Pius. There was a brief stint at Kendriya Vidyalaya, but after just a few months, I returned to St. Pius where my academic foundation really took shape. One of my earliest milestones came in the 8th grade at the District General Knowledge Quiz Competition. Competing against thousands of students, I won 1st Prize—a moment that quietly built my confidence and proved that curiosity could take you far regardless of your surroundings.

Driven by a desire to represent my peers, I threw my hat into the ring for school elections, won, and served as Headboy. Beyond books and elections, my world revolved around cricket. Almost every single evening belonged to the pitch. As team captain, I got to lead a great group of players, learning how to handle match situations, keep everyone aligned, and enjoy the game under a bit of pressure.

The biggest surprise, however, came at the very end of my hometown chapter. When the Class 10 CBSE results were declared, my parents were initially disappointed with my raw marks. I went to sleep that night feeling the weight of that disappointment. The next morning, I was woken up to pure shock: my parents were staring at the morning newspaper, where my name was printed right at the top as the CBSE District Topper.

Sheopur gave me my foundation: the humility of small-town roots, the everyday camaraderie of evening cricket, and the lesson that success often arrives when you least expect it.
